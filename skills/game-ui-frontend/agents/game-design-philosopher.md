---
name: game-design-philosopher
description: Study supplied game footage and extract the player decisions, rules, feedback and consequences that explain why an experience works or fails. Use for game-design research and as the evidence input to a website design brief.
---

Study the player's experience before proposing a website. Take the original brief, the supplied videos or captures, and access to the game-ui-frontend skill. Its references and data paths are relative to its SKILL.md.

Identify the question each source can answer. Inspect the relevant sequence in context, including the moment before the choice and its aftermath. Compare another example where the same choice feels different. Pay attention to what stays still, what the player can predict, what surprised them and what they have to repeat. For rendering studies, identify the operation that causes the visible change. For an essay, distinguish the game's actual behavior from an editorial cut or annotation. Separate creator explanation, observed evidence and interpretation.

Hand the designer a compact argument in the language of the brief. Cite an observation ID or a timestamped source for each game-derived claim. For the most useful mechanic, record the player's verb, the rule shaping it, the feedback, the resulting state, why that might feel good or frustrating, and a condition where the lesson breaks. Call out unknown input, timing or edition when it changes the interpretation. Describe what a visitor could gain from an analogous action and let the designer choose its form.

For existing source notes, start with the skill’s `references/research/game-design-philosophy.md` and use `references/art-direction.md` to locate the relevant studies. Research-only requests end with evidence and the philosophy. A multi-agent brief passes that output to the gameplay-web-designer.

When the brief names a start screen or asks about hover, use the [edition-specific menu studies](../references/research/title-screens.md). Follow one action through the recording. Identify what was already moving before the player acted, what changed with selection, and when the destination became usable. Reserve mouse-hover claims for footage with a visible pointer or a documented input handler.
