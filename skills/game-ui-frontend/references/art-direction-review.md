# Review the detail someone came for

Use this when a working screen is tidy and the brief still promises an experience the visitor cannot have. Read the commissioning person's words again. What would they lean in to see, handle themselves, or compare? Choose the moment where their answer would make the biggest difference. A quiet interface can deserve as much attention here as an elaborate one.

**Make the first draft answer a harder question.** Suppose a sound archivist wants listeners to hear how one room changes a voice. Two attractive recording cards let someone select a file. Can they hear the *same phrase* from the other end of the room while their place in the comparison holds? That question suggests an original control and a meaningful result. Get the phrase and the relationship between the recordings from the archive. If the owner instead needs visitors to license a known recording, a direct route to that recording and its terms can be the better design. Make the call from the brief. Ask the owner only when a missing fact would change it.

**Give a correction a visible consequence.** Replace “needs more polish” with a direction another person can inspect. Name what is anchored, which part can move, who owns the selected state and where the real content lands. Compare the resting state with intent, commitment and return. Check one seam or label that would give away a careless construction. Switch to a neighbour mid-transition. Then use the control again after reading the longest destination on a small phone. Apply the checks that could change this particular screen.

Follow the action beyond the button. The portfolio owner's cabinet brief gave physical switches a job, such as operating the ventilation or the lights. Cover the switch after using it. Can you tell what changed by looking at the cabinet? Use that question wherever the brief gives a control something to operate.

Try the ambiguous gesture. If a visitor can turn an object inside a draggable room, drag directly on the object, starting close to its link. Its reverse should come into view while the room stays put and the link stays unopened. Try an ordinary tap separately. If a paper edge is meant to flutter, look at it at reading distance with the rest of the scene present. Working animation code alone cannot tell you whether the paper is visible behind the desk. The useful fix might be moving the paper into view rather than increasing the motion. Keep the reading surface steady.

**Revise the idea as well as the finish.** When an owner rejects an earlier choice, carry the work they still value into the new version. Removing a dark backdrop needn't sacrifice the roster that sat on it. Keep the latest correction through hover, touch, return and neighbouring screens. If the change is in scope and the necessary content exists, make it and retry. Put the specific before/after result in the handoff. Keep these review questions out of the visitor's interface.

## Where this standard came from

The portfolio and Quipus are the skill creator's own sites. The first example paraphrases the portfolio owner's design revisions. The other two report journeys exercised on the public sites on 21 November 2026. They illustrate how precisely to review a decision. For a client's site, its subject supplies the material, and the [bundled game studies](research/essay-readings.md) supply the interaction references.

- **Nathan's portfolio.** The portfolio brief moved from a fighting-game roster to a globe. The feedback narrowed in on portraits fitting their cells at rest, a single portrait lifting on intent, and neighbouring grid lines meeting. Later feedback removed the dark screen behind it and asked for the particles to gather at the opened project. Those were revisions over time. A useful review freezes the actual tile at its join, changes target, then checks whether the opened work receives the same selection. The owner also asked for reachable bottom navigation on mobile and challenged the unused space behind it. Check the last item against the bottom controls at the end of a real scroll. [Portfolio](https://nathanhor.com/)
- **What the portfolio currently delivers.** Selecting Persephone leaves a smaller working globe beside footage of the board in a hand. Choose Game Frontend Skills directly from that globe and the right-hand space becomes a README and annotated file viewer. Back to globe enlarges the selector and restores focus to the last project. A hardware project and a software project each get the evidence a visitor needs to judge them. Try two items whose evidence genuinely differs before signing off on a selector. [Portfolio project globe](https://nathanhor.com/#projects)
- **Quipus.** Mark 2 has its own plinth in the city. Opening it puts a labelled device model and a photograph that establishes its scale within reach. Choosing microSD explains local recording and later upload. Choosing USB-C answers a different question about power. At 390 pixels wide, all five controls sit above the brief and the selected part survives the change of layout. To review an equivalent selector, trace the active label to its part, read the corresponding explanation, then change part. A neatly drawn line that ends at the wrong part is a design failure. These are observations of the live product site. [Quipus Mark 2](https://quipus-sales-rep-assistant.vercel.app/#mark-2) · [Earlier site observations](../examples/authored-sites.json)
