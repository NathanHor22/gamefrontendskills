## Use the visitor’s decision to art-direct the shot

In the narration about *Jet Set Radio Future*, HeavynSent connects the clothing to graffiti, extreme sports and 2000s futurism. That explains why the movement and the object in the character’s hand belong in the same picture. For a brief rooted in a real scene, identify who uses the space, what they carry and how they move there before choosing type or colour. [HeavynSent on the influences, 29:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9hkInwI3iqM&t=1773s)

**“Help people choose a coat to commission.”** Photograph the tailor’s three cuts on the same person, at the same distance, with shoulder and hem visible in every thumbnail. A cropped jacket and a long split coat should read differently even in greyscale. Keep the cut name next to each choice. Selecting the coat opens a larger side view of the split over a walking stride. It answers a question a front view cannot. Show the starting price alongside **Request a fitting** and let visitors open fabric and lining details. On a phone, keep the thumbnail choices and names together and place the selected view directly below. A still of the stride carries the same information with reduced motion.

**“Let returning clients book an alteration.”** A close photograph of the seam beside the alteration choices will do more work here. The decision depends on the alteration type and available date. The same brand can express its craft at a different scale because the visitor has a different job.
