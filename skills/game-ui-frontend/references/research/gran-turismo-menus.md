# Choosing where to go in Gran Turismo

The series makes room for two different speeds. Someone can linger with a car in motion, then move quickly between race types, tracks or dealerships. The clearest clue to a menu’s purpose is what changes as the current selection moves around it.

## A highlight can carry a decision across a cut

In GT3, watch the lettering at 0:51, 0:53 and 0:55. First Arcade Mode sits in amber over bright red motion. The whole background darkens. The selected lettering survives as the image falls away. When the blue submenu comes up, Arcade Mode is its heading and Single Race is already the active choice. A visitor only needs the selected word and a stable first action to understand what has happened. [RacingGameArchive, 0:51–1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ws1L7E1Fng&t=51s)

In GT4, follow New York at 6:03, Clubman Stage Route 5 at 6:07 and Seoul Central at 6:10. The selected thumbnail brightens. Each time the large view, name and track outline agree about the selection. The row retains its position throughout. This makes an unseen track easier to compare by both appearance and shape. The console capture includes an on-screen pointer, so the frames establish selection. They do not establish a mouse hovering over a web-style control. [AndroBench, 6:03–6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a2Lg9F0euF0&t=363s)

## When a map earns its place

The original PlayStation map groups makers, licences and a home base into different destinations. GT7’s resort serves a similar purpose. The map helps the player choose a building. Entering the used-car dealership changes the presentation completely, because comparing models needs prices and mileage that an island cannot show. The producer describes shopping for a first car as the beginning of progression. Sony’s GT5 manual likewise distinguishes the persistent career from a quick arcade race. Those different intentions justify offering two entrances. [GT7 State of Play](https://www.gran-turismo.com/sg/news/00_1796083.html) · [GT5 driver’s manual](https://cdn.us.playstation.com/pscomauth/groups/public/documents/webasset/gt5_ps3_manual_final.pdf)

GT Sport offers a caution for an animated home screen. Notice the same sunset scene at 5:32 with the top and side controls and at 5:35 with the controls gone. By 5:41 the film has changed location. The upload leaves the cause of this handoff open. Even if a game can afford an attract sequence, someone halfway through a website selection should keep their controls. The studio’s update notes also describe changes to startup footage over the game's lifetime. [Shadow Blaze, 5:32–5:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=61CiBrysDIU&t=332s) · [Polyphony Digital’s top-screen update](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gtsport/news/00_4140485.html)

GT PSP fits a garage into two rows of objects. Selecting a shelf changes its base to orange. A text label appears for the current item, and opening the collection replaces the shelf with photographs of the actual cars. This structure costs recognition time if the visitor has never seen the icons. On a phone, keep the short name inside every interactive shelf and let movement signal the current one. [Mark01766, 1:38–2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6tAdNmhG-mY&t=98s) · [Official PSP edition](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/products/gtpsp/)

## A website decision

For an open-studios weekend, let visitors choose among actual workshops on an illustrated street. Give each studio one legible sign-shaped button. Moving keyboard focus or a fine pointer to a sign changes the nearby workshop photograph. A tap, click or Enter opens that studio’s available sessions. Once inside, show the times and remaining places in plain, contained controls. A booking begins only when someone selects a session. Keep the chosen studio and its sign in focus on Back.

The street has to earn the space it occupies. If all participants share one room, use a compact set of named exhibitors instead. A direct studio URL should open its sessions with no drive through the street. Reduced motion should deliver the same photograph, schedule and place in the return flow.

**Evidence limits.** These are playheads in individual recordings of the identified games. The GT2 capture explicitly shows its Arcade Mode Disc. GT6 is a Polish-language recording and GT PSP a French-language recording. The clips establish visible navigation states and changes in imagery. They give no reliable physical press duration, input latency or easing measurements. The manufacturer’s manuals supply terminology and mode purposes.
