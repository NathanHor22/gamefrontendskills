# How Life is Strange keeps the coast alive behind its menu

The original game lets the player linger over the bay or act on one prompt. The coast then holds the menu together while the player moves between choices. Starting the story is the moment that takes the view away.

| Moment | What is visible |
| --- | --- |
| **Waiting at the title** | In the publisher’s recording, light breaks across the water, the pattern of surf changes and dark silhouettes cross the sky. At 0:02, 0:07, 0:12 and 0:24 the viewpoint still looks down the same coastline. The title occupies the sky and **Press Any Button** stays in the lower right. The publisher also released this scene as a ten-minute “moment of calm”. The length of the underlying animation loop is unknown. ## Applying it to a website

For a coastal photographer’s archive, open on a real location from the collection. Let water move in one part of the image. Put **Browse photographs** on a flat, ink-coloured contact-sheet tab where it remains legible as the water brightens. Reveal a pale paper edge on hover or keyboard focus and retract it on exit. Keep the label, hit area and other tabs steady. A touch activates the same choice immediately.

On activation, open the first contact sheet in the same place. Keep the shore visible while someone changes collections. Opening a photograph can then replace the scene with the print at reading size. **Back** should restore the collection, the focused photograph and the visitor’s place. With reduced motion, show a still shore and switch views directly. Move over several choices quickly, open a photograph, then press Back. The visible selection and restored focus should both identify that photograph without explanatory copy.
