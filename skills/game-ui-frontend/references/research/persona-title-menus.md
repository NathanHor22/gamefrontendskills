# How Persona moves from title to choice

**Persona 3 Reload** takes the visitor into a room before presenting its commands. **Persona 5 Royal** gives each command its own view of the city. The distinction matters on a website. A change of scene can announce that a visitor has entered a collection, or it can preview the item they are considering. Make clear which of those two things just happened.

## Two entrances, sampled closely

**Persona 3 Reload.** Between 0:07.00 and 0:08.25 the large prompt goes from filled letters to outlines and back. The figure at the window holds its place. At 0:08.75 the prompt is still present. By 0:09.00 it has cleared and the view is widening. Desks and other students enter the frame and the logo settles above them. A blue strip crosses the lower screen at 0:10.00. **New Game** appears by 0:10.25, **Load Game** by 0:10.50 and **Config** by 0:10.75. The strip resolves behind the selected command. This gives the eye somewhere to land after the move. The recording establishes idle animation and menu entry. It does not identify an input device. [Classroom entrance, 0:06–0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2JjHVOr58-w&t=6s)

**Persona 5 Royal.** At 0:12 the title and prompt share a night-time plaza. By 0:13 four commands occupy one horizontal row. A cyan paper-like plate marks **New Game**, moves to **Load Game** at 0:15, to the concealed command at 0:17.5, then to **Config** at 0:19.5. Each move restages the plaza behind the controls. The foreground figure, billboards and camera angle change, but the choices stay in reach. At 0:21.5 the plate is back on **New Game** and its earlier view returns. The row clears by 0:23.5. Nothing here identifies a pointer or proves where the final action leads. [Royal selection sequence, 0:12–0:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M65OgXqMs44&t=12s)

## A website decision

For an independent cinema, use the **Reload** approach to connect a view of the projection booth to the programme. Put **Find a screening** on a perforated ticket. On activation, reveal the programme immediately and widen the booth view around it. Move reading focus to the programme heading. Someone arriving through a film’s direct link should already see its screening times.

Use the **Royal** approach when the visitor can compare this week’s films. Keep their titles on fixed tickets. Hover or keyboard focus changes the still on the projection screen. The ticket stays under the pointer and the address stays put. Clicking, pressing Enter or tapping a film opens its performance times. Back restores the selected film and focus. Interrupt a preview if the visitor reaches the next ticket before it settles. On a phone, one tap opens the times. With reduced motion, exchange the film stills directly.
