# What a start screen asks the visitor to do

The useful question is what happens after someone acts. An offline RPG can open a list immediately. An online game may need the player to choose a server. A rhythm game can use its first prompt to establish the controls for the session. These sequences come from decoded frames of the named games. Where a recording waits at the title, the follow-on flow is identified separately.

## One object, one set of choices

**Final Fantasy VII Rebirth, PS5.** At 1:00 a narrow blue start prompt sits below the logo on black. At 1:03 the title dims and a save-data check appears. At 1:05 a spotlight picks out the Buster Sword on the right. Commands sit on the left. A blue band with a bright point holds **New Game**, then **Load Game** at 1:07. By 1:09 **Options** has opened over the same sword. Categories occupy the first panel and their settings fill the second at 1:10. A difficulty-value picker opens over its row at 1:12. A website can likewise keep a chosen object in view while its options expand beside it. Give the active row a solid, shaped hit area so its limits survive glare or reduced contrast. [First startup and options, 1:00–1:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6XERW-bbL2o&t=60s)

**Final Fantasy XVI, PS5.** The menu is already available at 0:22. Two illustrated figures fill the upper centre, with warm sparks on the left and cool glints on the right. Four commands sit beneath the title. A white hand and horizontal red light pick out **Continue**. The particles vary at later samples while the selected command and composition hold. This recording stays on one choice. For a returning visitor, put **Resume** on a readable plate in the quiet part of the image. Keep identifying motion out of the label so its meaning survives a still frame. [Title idle, 0:22–1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KK4i9Jy1rIo&t=22s)

A separate first-play recording shows version 1.02 on the title. **New Game** is selected at 1:25. By 1:30, **System Settings** pairs the brightness slider with three preview images. **Gameplay Focus** follows at 1:33, then a final summary with a single **Go** button at 1:39. For a site that needs initial configuration, show the effect of a choice beside its control and carry that choice into the final summary. [Fresh-game setup, 1:25–1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F0jcrFJW4I0&t=85s)

**Metaphor: ReFantazio.** The white logo arches around a cyan figure in a painted city. At 0:43 **Press Any Button** sits just below it. At 0:45 the same space holds three commands, with a red brush-shaped fill behind **Load Game**. It appears behind **New Game** at 1:05 and **System** at 1:39. Traffic and people can pass through the picture while the row stays put. A brush-shaped backing makes a good portfolio control when the work itself is made in ink. Give each destination a permanent footprint. Let the ink change on focus. The footage shows the selection change but does not identify mouse hover. ## When the next destination depends on the player

**Phantasy Star Online, Dreamcast.** The original, dated 2000 on screen, holds the planet, luminous orbit and central title in place. The cyan start prompt is visible at 0:23, dim at 0:29, bright at 0:34 and absent at 0:41. SEGA’s manual documents the following screen. Start opens the menu. New Game leads to character creation, Continue selects a memory card, and Options and Website are separate choices. On a site with saved projects, label the two paths **Create a project** and **Open a project**. Each label can tell the visitor which choice they will make next. [Dreamcast title, 0:15–0:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l1mtXl-uwkQ&t=15s) · [SEGA’s Dreamcast manual, Starting a Game](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/318245/Sega-Phantasy-Star-Online.html?page=3)

**Phantasy Star Online 2: New Genesis.** An action movie precedes the title. At 2:40 the title sits over a bright valley with a dark horizontal prompt strip immediately below. At 3:00 and 4:18 the prompt still occupies the same place. SEGA’s Japanese PC guide describes the next choices as ship/server and login, followed by character selection or creation. The recording demonstrates the title’s composition and the guide supplies the route. For a multi-workspace site, a readily visible workspace selector gives a welcome screen an actual job. [Valley title, 2:40–4:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F1_Qvt6kY8g&t=160s) · [SEGA’s PC start guide](https://pso2.jp/players/manual/gamestart/start/start/)

**Honkai: Star Rail, version 3.7.** The train crosses the field on a diagonal with **Start Game** centred above a rounded region selector reading **Europe**. The utilities sit separately on the right. At 0:09 both central controls remain. By 0:12 they clear. By 0:15 the view looks along the locomotive toward purple light, with a broad **Click to Start** strip at the bottom. The recording ends there. A site that depends on region can make that choice beside its first action and acknowledge it through a scene change. A site ready to show its content can open it on the first activation. [Versioned login sequence, 0:01–0:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n-FkZQV7E74&t=1s)

## When the control teaches the activity

**Taiko no Tatsujin: Drum ’n’ Fun!, Switch.** The Western title places **L + R Start** between the drum mascots and logo. In a separately recorded, Japanese-branded *Nintendo Switch Version!* running in English, the same prompt appears at 0:30. Fireworks fill the screen at 0:35, a festival scene appears at 0:36, then the mode stack lands at 0:37. The selected mode expands into a framed panel while the other choices remain compact. The Western multiplayer capture begins in song selection. At 0:03, 0:06 and 0:09 different songs occupy the same enlarged central slot. Its yellow border and difficulty information travel with the selection. At 0:20 two player markers share a row of difficulty tiles. At 0:28 a rules list replaces them.
