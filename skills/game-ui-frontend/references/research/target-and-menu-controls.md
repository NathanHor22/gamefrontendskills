# How games point, select and open

Choose the reference by what the visitor needs to know. A ring answers “which one?”, a bearing answers “which way?”, and a strip behind a verb answers “what can I do?”. The recording times below identify frames. The website gestures are original reconstructions. Use the visitor’s actual landmarks, artefacts and verbs to draw them.

## Keep the selected subject in sight

**Dragon Ball Z: Tenkaichi Tag Team, PSP.** At 3:07.6 two thin yellow circles surround Raditz’s torso. Small wedges at three and nine o’clock point inwards. His name and health sit above and to the right. At 3:10.9 he is much smaller and farther right, yet the rings still enclose him at a readable screen size. At 3:11.5–3:11.9 the stage darkens and both the contour and wedges stay visible. The footage resolves the tracking, layering and orientation. It does not establish a repeat interval for the pulse described in the brief. [Tag Team, 3:07–3:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgKACS46XII&t=187s)

**Build it.** On a conservation studio’s specimen plate, give each selectable detail a named hotspot. Mount one shared pair of rings on the chosen detail. Keep their diameter in screen pixels as the photograph zooms. Give each wedge its own short translation along the horizontal radius. On acquisition, draw the wedges in, let them settle, and hold both outlines. Changing detail moves the marker and updates the specimen name together. One pulse is enough to announce a new lock. A steady double contour communicates the same selection with motion reduced.

## Give the arrow a destination

**Spider-Man, PlayStation, 2000.** Two effects coexist. At 5:29.50 a large translucent red triangle covers Spider-Man as he climbs. By 5:29.75 it is smaller and farther right. At 5:30.00 it sits at the right edge. Separately, the small pointer inside the fixed Spider-Compass changes colour: yellow at 5:29.25, red at 5:29.75, yellow at 5:30.25 and red at 5:30.75. This repeats a third time as the bearing changes. The manual assigns route guidance to the lower-right compass. The edge triangle’s appearance alone does not establish its trigger. The PSP compilation shows *Spider-Man 3* and *Spider-Man 2*, distinct releases. [PS1 rooftop, 5:28–5:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YkBwKGsPU9c&t=328s) · [Original manual, “The Spider-Compass”](https://www.videogamemanual.com/ps1/Spider-Man%20%28USA%29.pdf)

**Build it.** In a panoramic exhibition, keep the chosen artwork’s name available while it is out of view. Derive the arrow’s angle from the artwork’s position relative to the viewport centre. Intersect that ray with an inset viewport edge. Pulse a child inside the rotated marker so scale never changes the bearing. On arrival, hand the cue to a contour around the artwork and retire the edge arrow. Make the named destination reachable by keyboard or tap in one action.

## Draw only the information the instrument can tell you

**Metal Gear Solid and Peace Walker.** The PSP compilation’s *Peace Walker* shot goes over the shoulder and shows a red targeting mark over a soldier by 2:35. Konami’s separate *Peace Walker* manual shows a circular **Surround Indicator** with a centre mark and a coloured sector. The sector reports the direction and proximity of sound. The original *Metal Gear Solid* radar diagram instead uses a square field, sparse architecture, dots for actors and cones for vision. A cone has an origin and a direction. It never stands in for a clickable button. [PSP footage, 2:31–2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgKACS46XII&t=151s) · [Konami’s Surround Indicator diagram](https://metalgear.konami.net/manual/mc2/mgspw/xbox/en/page10.html) · [Konami’s original-game radar key](https://metalgear.konami.net/manual/mc1/mgs1/pc/en/page08.html)

**Build it.** For a venue guide, put the foyer at the origin and draw the few walls between it and the exhibits. A circular mask, a pinned **You are here** point and a sector pointing to the selected room combine these studies. Use actual plan coordinates for each room. With north up and SVG coordinates increasing downwards, an arrow drawn pointing right takes the angle `atan2(roomY - foyerY, roomX - foyerX)`. Use one coordinate system for plan and pointer. Selecting a second room must change the bearing and its accessible name. A room at the origin needs an arrival state.

## Give a command its own silhouette

**Persona 3 Reload.** At 0:07–0:08.25 the title prompt shifts between outlined and filled letters. After it clears, the classroom opens up. At 0:10 a blue strip crosses the lower view. *New Game*, *Load Game* and *Config* arrive in sequence, and the strip rests behind one choice. The combat still uses different geometry: short italic verbs cross a disc, with the larger arc behind the figure. **Persona 3 Portable** has its own language. At 2:14 a light capsule encloses *Load Game*. Its battle excerpt at 8:43 uses an outlined marker over an enemy. None of these recordings demonstrates mouse hover. [Reload entrance, 0:07–0:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2JjHVOr58-w&t=7s) · [Official Reload command still and breakdown](persona-composition.md) · [Portable title, 2:04–2:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2LNePf0LqE&t=124s) · [Portable battle, 8:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgKACS46XII&t=523s)

**Build it.** On a record archive, let **Play side A** cross the label of an original paper sleeve. Enclose the full verb in a coloured plate. The sleeve corner is the pivot for a detached paper tab. Keyboard focus or pointer entry unfolds the tab beyond the plate. Press seats it against the sleeve. Playback changes the verb to **Pause side A** when audio actually starts. On exit, tuck the tab under the sleeve from its current angle. The hit area and live text stay put throughout.

## Use depth to change what can be read or chosen

**Indiana Jones and the Staff of Kings, PSP.** At 0:02 an open journal lies diagonally amongst objects on a surface. By 0:04 the viewpoint has moved close and squared up the spread. At 0:05 the journal title, bottom commands and right-edge page arrow are visible. At 0:19–0:22 the writing fades to the last few lines and the available arrow moves to the left edge. In the supplied compilation the camera instead follows Indy past a boulder. The longer recording shows the actual menu transition. [Journal approach, 0:01–0:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3liRcmCNRc&t=1s) · [Change spread, 0:19–0:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3liRcmCNRc&t=19s) · [Compilation, 4:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgKACS46XII&t=241s)

**Build it.** Let an archive visitor open a field notebook from the worktable. Keep the spine as the transform origin while the cover squares up to the viewport. At reading distance, render the actual entry as selectable text. Inside the notebook, enable the next or previous leaf only where one exists. Back returns to the same notebook on the table. On a narrow screen, open directly to a readable single page.

**Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, PSP.** The compilation shows a spell illuminating an object in a forest. The character menu in a longer capture is more instructive for selection: three full figures share a stage. At 1:27 a faceted pointer hangs above Harry, at 1:29 it is above Hermione and at 1:31 it is over Ron. The figures turn their heads or shift their weight while the lower command changes from *Select* to *Accept*. On the later equipment screen the chosen figure stands in front of the others and the card slots take the focus. [Forest, 3:25–3:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgKACS46XII&t=205s) · [Character selection, 1:27–1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zlLOrCokBw&t=87s) · [Equipment handoff, 10:27–10:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zlLOrCokBw&t=627s)

**Build it.** A puppet-maker can stage three puppets on one rail. Attach one faceted tag just above the currently inspected puppet. Let a change of focus turn that puppet slightly on its support so its joint becomes visible. Keep all three hit areas stationary. Opening its entry brings the selected puppet forward and reveals the joint study and a commission action. A direct link should open that entry immediately. With motion reduced, exchange the settled poses. Depth earns its place by revealing the construction.
