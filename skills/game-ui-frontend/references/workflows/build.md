# Build a website from a game interaction

Start with the [short brief in the skill](../../SKILL.md#when-someone-says-build-my-website-with-this). Ask for the missing decisions in one exchange. After the reply, make the site. If the user requested a new opening for an existing site, wire it to the real routes and keep the rest of the site within the requested scope.

## Choose what the visitor gets to do

Use [one real example of the work](../art-direction.md#design-from-one-real-example) before picking a game. For a collection, establish how its owner groups the pieces and what deserves a closer look. For a tool, follow an input through the work it performs to an output someone can inspect. Use the [opening studies](../research/title-screens.md) or [catalogue](../research/catalog.md) to find the relevant decision, which might be browsing, preparation, work in progress or a result. When the user names a game, resolve its edition in the bundle. If it’s absent, identify the studied analogue and what it shares with the requested experience.

| What the brief needs | What happened in the game | What that could become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Let someone linger in a place | In the original *Life is Strange* PC menu, the handwritten choice under the pointer grows while the bay keeps its framing. [Coastal menu study](../research/life-is-strange-title.md) | A field recordist's coastline can stay in view while a labelled tape button responds to focus. Choosing the tape opens its actual recording and transcript. |
| Let someone audition alternatives | *Persona 5 Royal* keeps four commands in place as the selected plate and the plaza composition change. [Royal selection sequence](../research/persona-title-menus.md) | An independent cinema can keep film titles fixed on ticket-shaped buttons while the projector changes stills. Choosing a ticket opens that film’s screening times. |
| Let someone explore and retrace their steps | In *Gran Turismo* on PSP an illuminated shelf leads to the collection and an individual car. Back restores the shelf grid. [Handheld shelf study](../research/gran-turismo-menus.md) | A ceramics archive can give each glaze a shelf. Carry the chosen glaze into the pot details, then return to that shelf with focus intact. |
| Give a choice consequences | *Warframe* leaves neighbouring nodes visible when a mission brief opens and exposes relevant preparation in Navigation. [Mission selection study](../research/warframe-navigation.md) | Selecting a workshop on a studio plan shows its actual times and requirements. Carry the session the visitor picks into the existing reservation flow. |

These are examples of reasoning from a source. Find another bundled moment when it fits the brief better. Use a second study when it answers a different design question, such as how the destination opens. Record the edition and observation ID or documented playhead in the build notes you give the user. Keep the observed behaviour and your proposed web interaction distinct. A game screenshot can support a composition while leaving its hover timing open.

## Let the start screen do useful work

Compose the first viewport around something that belongs to this particular site. Give the visitor one readily identifiable action on a visibly shaped button. Choose what can move while they linger, what reacts to browsing, and what changes only when they commit. Spend the strongest transition on a meaningful change of context. A compulsory “Press Start” that simply reveals a conventional homepage adds a click.

Follow one item all the way through. Which performance did they choose? Do the venue and times on the destination belong to that show? Can they come back, pick the other show and get its details? Carry the same subject, selection and control language through the requested pages. Source the wider design from the bundled studies too. Keep forms, reading and navigation straightforward wherever they already serve the visitor.

## Deliver the working site

Use the project’s stack, content and existing routes. In an empty project, a standalone HTML file with local styles and script can be enough. Use original or supplied artwork. Where content is genuinely missing, make sample material easy to replace and identify it in the handoff. A booking or purchase confirmation requires a real service result.

Give a mouse and a keyboard the same preview. Let a phone visitor reach the destination with a tap. Keep each hit area still when its face animates. Back after browsing should restore the choice. A shared link opens the destination immediately and preserves browser history back to the referring page. Include a visible route to the entrance. Reduced motion keeps the same content and actions available. Use the [verification workflow](verify.md) where browser tools are available.

Test the detail the design promised. On a photo board, enlarge a print, inspect a second region of your choice, then fit the image and return to its place. For a software demo, run two inputs and compare their actual outputs. In a connected product check the outcome against the service response. Report the working path, the specific game moment that shaped it and what you verified. Keep evidence, intake questions and critique in the conversation, outside the visitor-facing interface.
